# Pane materials

Pane surfaces, glass ownership, shadows, and rendering boundaries.

See [[lat.md/tests/pane-materials-tests|Test coverage]].

## Pane materials

Browser panes use rounded native Liquid Glass panels on every supported macOS version. Focus uses a brighter title plus a configurable transparency, outline, glow, or title tint treatment.

[BrowserView.swift](../../Sources/Browse/BrowserView.swift) uses None by default. In Transparency mode, inactive panes, including chrome and page content, receive a configurable black overlay (8% by default). None disables inactive-pane dimming, outlines, glow, and title tint while preserving ordinary pane shadows and the standard title emphasis. Outline and Outline glow instead mark the focused pane without dimming other panes. The outline has a color, independent gap, and thickness. Adjustment ranges are [[Sources/BrowseCore/PaneHighlight.swift#PaneHighlight#outlineGapRange|0–2.5 pt gap]] and [[Sources/BrowseCore/PaneHighlight.swift#PaneHighlight#outlineWidthRange|0.5–2 pt thickness]]. The ring expands outward from the actual pane bounds; changing modes or either dimension never insets the pane or changes its WebView size. It draws beyond the scroll canvas, subject to the window boundary and neighboring pane interiors. Glow centers a colored halo on the focused pane’s silhouette without the ordinary shadow’s vertical offset, with a separate color, 0–100% intensity for shadow opacity, and independent 0–24 pt spread for blur radius. It adds no border stroke or inward glow. Title tint adds a chosen color at 0–100% opacity behind only the active pane’s title-row controls, over its existing material. It works on web, start, and error pages without tinting page content. On web pages it covers the entire shared title-and-find header; inactive headers retain their normal appearance. [[Sources/Browse/PaneShadow.swift#PaneShadowView]] draws both outline and glow on the window decoration layer outside the native pane and canvas clips. Decorations ignore hit testing and accessibility. Title-tint focus changes fade over 100 ms unless Reduce Motion is enabled. [[lat.md/architecture/settings#UI preferences]] owns these controls.

[BrowserView.swift](../../Sources/Browse/BrowserView.swift) places the pane canvas over the shared [[lat.md/architecture/sidebar#Sidebar materials|app base material]]. Pane chrome and built-in pages reveal the panel material; web pages keep their own rendering. [PaneShadow.swift](../../Sources/Browse/PaneShadow.swift) draws broad, low-opacity black pane shadows on a separate window background layer outside the clipped scroll canvases. The focused pane in Outline glow mode replaces its offset black shadow with the configured centered colored halo; focus and preference changes redraw the layer. It follows visible native pane geometry, including scroll and resize updates, with drawing space sized for the full blur. [[Sources/Browse/PaneShadow.swift#PaneShadowView#drawEdgeLight]] adds a neutral half-point outer edge with a faint 1.25-point shoulder to every pane. Its opacity rises smoothly through the top 48 points, joining the glass header’s side highlights without adding a bright top rim. This passive separation is independent of the focus style and consumes no layout space. The edge has a fixed 3% intensity, with a proportionally softer shoulder and no user setting. Any previously saved experimental edge intensity is ignored. Focus outlines are drawn after shadows and edge lighting, and pane interiors are cleared after all decorations are drawn, preserving translucent materials and preventing shadows on neighboring panes. Hidden panes cast no shadow, and the shadow layer ignores input and accessibility. The window shell retains six-point outer margins while page scrolling remains clipped. [[lat.md/tests/pane-materials-tests#Pane shadow boundaries]] covers drawing beyond a clipped canvas and following moved or hidden panes. Pane content fills its rounded native clip with no additional rim or border inset. Pane body materials retain a 50% black scrim. Headers offer Regular/Clear glass with independent tint and darkening, or an opaque Solid color gradient. Defaults remain Regular, no tint, and 50% darkening. Their background sits beneath the controls; it does not dim text or web pages.

On macOS 26 and later, each live web page extends behind its compact title row. [WebSurfaceHost.swift](../../Sources/Browse/WebSurfaceHost.swift) provides one shared background behind the title and any open find controls; opening search extends that surface downward. Glass styles reveal scrolling page colors, while Solid color completely covers them. The combined title and find height sets WebKit's obscured content inset, keeping fixed and sticky site elements below the controls. Start pages and errors retain the stacked header layout. Stacked headers and page bodies have separate backgrounds, so header customization does not alter built-in page bodies or apply darkening twice. [[Sources/Browse/BrowserView.swift#PaneBodyBackground]] extends the body glass upward behind the header before clipping it to the body, hiding the body's own top rim so it cannot create a second line below the header. The mini player's controls use the same body background.

Solid headers use [[Sources/Browse/GlassAppearance.swift#SolidHeaderBackground]] for native web headers and [[Sources/Browse/BrowserView.swift#PaneChromeBackground]] for stacked and mini-player headers. Both use the chosen sRGB color with a slight white blend at the top and a slight black blend at the bottom. The fill is opaque and ignores glass tint, darkening, and rim clipping. Native glass is hidden in this mode; controls stay in a stable sibling container, preserving find focus and geometry across material switches. Pane bodies and the window retain their own materials.

Each web view fills its pane's viewport and scrolls its document internally through WebKit. Page content height does not size the web view or the pane. The outer canvas scrolls only when the split layout's minimum dimensions exceed the available window area.

The complete pane, including its outer glass background, header, and page, shares a rounded, layer-backed AppKit container. Clipping is applied after the pane background so its glass compositing layers cannot extend outside the silhouette. The header glass also has rounded top corners, and the clip follows pane resizing. [[Sources/Browse/WebSurfaceHost.swift#PaneHeaderGlass]] and stacked headers in [[Sources/Browse/BrowserView.swift#PaneChromeBackground]] extend the glass beyond the visible header and clip away its reflective rims. Top and bottom clipping follow [[Sources/Browse/BrowserStore.swift#BrowserStore#paneHeaderTopRimClipping]] and [[Sources/Browse/BrowserStore.swift#BrowserStore#paneHeaderBottomRimClipping]]. The live pane's backing material uses the same top clipping so its rim cannot show through the header. Side highlights remain; controls, header height, and WebKit's inset use the visible bounds. Glass headers use the native rim without a custom separator line; Solid color headers have no glass rims.

[[Sources/Browse/WebSurfaceHost.swift#WebSurfaceHost#updateWebKitHeaderEffect]] hides WebKit's own top scroll-pocket view when Bend supplies an overlaid header. WebKit can activate its blur after reparenting, doubling the material beneath the native glass. A guarded optional WebKit selector obtains only that effect view; layout reapplies suppression without changing page pixels, scroll position, or obscured insets. If the selector is unavailable, native behavior remains. [[lat.md/tests/pane-materials-tests#Header glass after window transfer]] covers repeated transfers and scrolling.

The web view has an additional native clip around its composited content: all corners under the glass header, or only the bottom corners in the stacked layout. Nested SwiftUI content inherits the pane's environment through its native host.
