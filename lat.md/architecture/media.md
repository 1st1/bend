# Media playback

Sidebar media controls, source ownership, and exclusive playback.

See [[lat.md/tests/media-tests|Test coverage]].

## Sidebar media player

[SidebarMediaPlayer.swift](../../Sources/Browse/SidebarMediaPlayer.swift) places a native Now Playing card below the tab list, controlling media in foreground or background panes. Playback controls preserve tab selection; choosing a source reveals its pane.

[MediaSessionBridge.swift](../../Sources/Browse/MediaSessionBridge.swift) installs one document-start script per content controller in the page world, including subframes. It forwards native Media Session action registrations unchanged while retaining callbacks for generic play, pause, previous, and next controls. Audio/video elements, including dynamically inserted players, open shadow roots, and off-DOM players started through the native play method, provide fallback play and pause. The play wrapper preserves the original return value and exceptions. Detached players remain resumable while paused and are discarded when ended, failed, or emptied; removing a previously attached player still retires it. Muted autoplay alone does not create a player. Sites declaring a playing Media Session can expose controls without a visible media element. Unsupported buttons remain disabled; this does not use site-specific selectors or simulated system keys.

Track titles and artists come from Media Session metadata, falling back to the active element's accessible label or title, then the document title and site. Playback events and metadata assignments send deduplicated updates. While a frame has an active or resumable session, [PageMediaController.swift](../../Sources/Browse/PageMediaController.swift) refreshes it every two seconds to detect in-place metadata changes and detached frames. Idle documents have no refresh timer. The card uses the pane favicon, with no separate artwork download.

[[Sources/Browse/MediaSessionBridge.swift#MediaSessionBridge#script]] qualifies finite audio/video only when its duration exceeds seven seconds. Short clips, including loops, cannot create a card, replace a longer source in the same frame, or claim exclusive playback from another pane. Unknown-duration and live players qualify after more than seven seconds of accumulated playback; HTML playback excludes pauses, buffering, seeking, and inaudible media. One native deadline per pending frame refreshes qualification without idle polling or reliance on throttled background-page timers. Qualification survives pauses; loading a new HTML resource resets its clock, and known short durations override prior qualification. [[Sources/Browse/PageMediaController.swift#PageMediaController#receive]] removes duration-filtered sources immediately instead of retaining the track-transition placeholder. [[lat.md/tests/media-tests#Short media filtering]] and [[lat.md/tests/media-tests#Unknown media duration qualification]] cover these boundaries.

The card always reserves its progress bar and time row; finite-duration media shows elapsed and total time. [MediaProgressSlider.swift](../../Sources/Browse/MediaProgressSlider.swift) draws a thin rounded track with a light filled portion and no separate thumb, matching the macOS volume bar. Native cell tracking and slider accessibility remain available. The control owns mouse routing so AppKit’s internal hosted input view cannot bypass drag boundaries. Arrow-key and accessibility adjustments seek in five-second steps and commit immediately; dragging commits on release. HTML media supplies current time, duration, and playback rate; handler-only players can supply Media Session position state. Native interpolation keeps the visible bar moving between existing two-second refreshes without extra page polling, and stops during pause, seeking, or reported buffering. Unknown or infinite durations show an empty, disabled track with dash time labels. Missing timing between tracks keeps the same bar and time-row geometry, preventing the card and playback controls from jumping. Seeking uses the site’s seek-to handler when registered, otherwise a seekable HTML media element; native and frame-side checks reject invalid targets. Scrubbing sends one seek on release and keeps commands bound to their source pane.

The card shares browser-pane chrome: a fixed-height glass header follows the pane header appearance and rim-clipping settings, with the favicon beside a single-line, tail-truncated title and a dismiss button. Its [[Sources/Browse/BrowserView.swift#PaneChromeBackground]] applies the shared top- and bottom-rim clipping amounts while preserving side highlights, matching [[lat.md/architecture/pane-materials#Pane materials]]. [[Sources/Browse/BrowserView.swift#PaneBodyBackground]] hides the controls surface's own top rim, leaving only the title header's adjustable bottom rim at their boundary. Clipping changes apply live without changing card geometry. Centered artist/site metadata and playback controls sit below. Left and bottom insets use the pane gap; the adjacent canvas supplies the right-side gap, with no extra trailing padding.

Only the card's dismiss button hides when the pointer leaves; hovering anywhere in the card reveals it. Its layout space stays reserved, and it cannot receive clicks or accessibility focus while hidden. Playback and source-picker controls stay visible. The dismiss fade matches the pane toolbar's 100 ms transition and respects Reduce Motion.

Messages are page-controlled input. Native routing uses WebKit's originating WebView and frame, bounds strings and session counts, strips control and directional formatting characters, and accepts only supported commands. Commands use structured arguments, target the recorded frame, and check its document and player identities before invoking a callback. Main-document changes, frame departure, WebView replacement, process termination, and closure discard obsolete sessions. The page-world bridge grants no native capability beyond reporting player state; media commands originate from native controls.

[WindowMediaCoordinator.swift](../../Sources/Browse/WindowMediaCoordinator.swift) selects the most recently started playing source, retaining paused sources for resuming. Selection stays with the current source through pauses and metadata changes until it is removed, dismissed, explicitly changed, or another source starts playing; changing browser tabs does not change the source. A compact picker to the left of the centered playback controls shows the selected favicon, roughly three quarters of a second favicon fading out, and a dropdown chevron. Its capsule hover profile and semantic foreground states match the playback buttons. The button favicons are grayscale at rest and return to their original colors on hover or press, using the shared hover animation and Reduce Motion behavior. Dropdown rows retain their full-color favicons. [MediaSourceMenu.swift](../../Sources/Browse/MediaSourceMenu.swift) builds native menu rows with 16-point favicons, a fallback music icon, and a checkmark for the selected source. When the public image-visibility setter exists, [[Sources/Browse/MediaSourceMenu.swift#MediaSourceMenu#makeMenu]] invokes it through guarded key-value coding to request visible images. This preserves favicons on macOS 27 without requiring its SDK declarations; macOS 26 uses ordinary native menu images. The dropdown and favicon preview order sources by most recent playback start or explicit selection; metadata updates and background refreshes preserve that order. [[lat.md/tests/media-tests#Media source menu images]] checks native image visibility and selection routing. At very narrow sidebar widths it moves to its own row to keep controls separate; choosing a source selects its browser tab and focuses its pane without changing playback. The close button dismisses the whole panel, retaining all sources and leaving playback unchanged. Refreshes, metadata changes, and already-playing sources cannot reveal a replacement card. A new playback start reopens the panel with all current sources still available. Clicking the title reveals its owning pane. Live pane transfers move ownership to the destination window while retaining the same player. Command failure offers a return to the page rather than claiming success. Volume remains controlled by the page; Bend does not expose a volume button or send volume commands.

Closing a pane or navigating to a native Bend page detaches its observer and pauses its media. An inactive media report retains its source and last metadata for up to two seconds to cover track replacement, with commands disabled and progress frozen. Resumed state cancels expiry; navigation, frame departure, controller reset, and invalid frame refresh still remove the source immediately. ## Exclusive media playback

[MediaPlaybackFocus.swift](../../Sources/Browse/MediaPlaybackFocus.swift) gives newly started playback priority and pauses media in other panes, including background tabs and other Bend windows.

Browser stores share their root store's playback coordinator. It retains controllers weakly, so live pane transfers keep their registration and do not count as a new playback start. A playing transition claims ownership, clears any old explicit card selection in that window, and pauses competing panes through their standard pause handlers and known HTML media elements. Native WebKit pause is the fallback for players without usable handlers. Pausing or closing the winner does not resume an older player automatically.

Native ownership revisions invalidate queued work after a newer start. Each frame also reports a playback revision; a delayed pause checks that revision and its document/player identity inside JavaScript before acting. [[lat.md/tests/media-tests#Exclusive media playback]] covers handoff within and between windows and rejection of an obsolete pause. [[lat.md/tests/media-tests#HTML media fallback controls]] verifies interruption pauses the actual audio element.
